Chapter 2: The Heat of Flamingo

The drive from the clouded peaks of the Central Valley to the sun-baked coastline of Guanacaste was a brutal five-hour crawl behind logging trucks and tourist buses. By the time Rojas parked his Land Cruiser near the white sands of Playa Flamingo, his shirt was clinging to his back. The air here was heavy with salt and the smell of fried red snapper from the beachside sodas. 

He wasn’t here for the view. The red clay on Elias Delgado’s expensive loafers had narrowed the search grid, but the fine, crushed white sand under his fingernails had pinpointed it. Flamingo was a playground for the rich—foreign developers, offshore bankers, and cartel accountants who wanted to blend in with the yacht crowd. 

Rojas walked into a dimly lit cantina at the edge of the marina. Behind the bar was 'El Gato' Herrera, a former OIJ informant who traded in secrets instead of cocaine. 

"Rojas," Gato said, pouring a shot of Cacique without being asked. "You look like a man who brought the rain with him."

"I need a ghost, Gato. Elias Delgado."

Gato’s hand paused, the bottle hovering over the glass. "Delgado is a big wolf, Teo. What do you want with him?"

"He's a dead wolf. Found him face down in a coffee plantation this morning. But he was here last night."

Gato wiped the bar down with a dirty rag, his eyes scanning the empty cantina. He leaned in close over the worn mahogany. "He was here. Rented Villa Cascadas up on the north ridge. But he wasn't doing Los Lobos business. He was meeting with some gringos in linen suits. Real estate types. They were arguing about a land permit down near the protected turtle reserves."

Rojas downed the shot. The burning in his throat matched the sudden sharp instinct in his gut. A mob accountant arguing over eco-reserves? It didn't fit the profile of a cartel war. Delgado was stepping out on his bosses.

"Thanks, Gato," Rojas muttered, tossing a crumbled bill on the counter. "Keep your ears open."
