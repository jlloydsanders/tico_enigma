Chapter 3: The Burner

Villa Cascadas was an architectural marvel of glass and teak wood, perched precariously on a cliff overlooking the churning Pacific. Rojas didn't bother looking for a key. He retrieved a rusted pry bar from the trunk of his Land Cruiser and popped the sliding glass door off its tracks with practiced ease. 

The air conditioning inside hit him like a physical blow. The place was immaculate. No signs of a struggle. No blood on the pristine white rugs. If Delgado was taken from here, he went quietly. Or he was dead before he ever left the driveway.

Rojas began his search systematically, tearing through the minimalist furniture. The bedroom closet held three bespoke suits, all empty. The master bathroom smelled of expensive cologne and sea salt. It was in the kitchen where Rojas found the anomaly. 

Behind a sleek, stainless-steel espresso machine, wedged tight against the marble backsplash, was a cheap, plastic pre-paid cell phone. A burner.

Delgado, a man who moved millions through encrypted servers for the cartel, wouldn't use a fifty-dollar disposable phone unless he was hiding something from his own people. 

Rojas grabbed a pair of latex gloves from his trench coat pocket and powered the phone on. It wasn't locked. There was only one number in the call history, dialed three times the previous evening. He memorized the number, then checked the text messages. There was only one, received at 8:00 PM, roughly four hours before the coroner estimated Delgado's death. 

It read: "El trato se cancela. The permit is denied. Bring the ledger to the casino."

Rojas slipped the phone into a plastic evidence bag. Salazar was wrong. The Culebras gang didn't kill Elias Delgado. Delgado was trying to buy his way out of the mob with a massive, legitimate real estate deal—and his new partners had decided a bullet was cheaper than a contract payout.

As Rojas turned to leave the kitchen, the distinct, metallic clack of a pump-action shotgun chambering a round echoed from the villa's hallway.

"Put your hands on your head, old man," a voice commanded in heavily accented Spanish.
